Roofing is a phone business run by people who can't be on the phone. The crew lead is on a roof, the owner is at a permit office or an insurance adjuster meeting, and the homeowner with a leak is calling the next roofer on Google Maps.

Why roofers lose more calls than most trades

  • Hands-on work all day. On a roof you can't take a call safely, so calls go to voicemail during peak hours.
  • Storm-driven spikes. Florida hurricane season runs June 1 to November 30, and summer afternoon thunderstorms bring leak calls in bursts.
  • Homeowners shop the list. A leaking roof means calling several companies at once, and most callers who reach voicemail don't leave a message.
  • Speed wins. Harvard Business Review found that companies replying to a lead within an hour were nearly 7 times more likely to qualify it than companies replying later.

Missed call text back vs. the alternatives

  • Voicemail: free, but most homeowners hang up and call the next roofer.
  • Answering service: a person picks up, but usually takes a message and doesn't book. You still have to call back.
  • Office manager: works until the phones spike after a storm, and costs a full salary.
  • Missed call text back: replies in seconds at any hour, qualifies the job and books it. Texting also suits homeowners who are at work.

How it works

  1. A call goes unanswered. You are on a roof, on a ladder or on another call.
  2. The homeowner gets a text within seconds from your business number: “Sorry we missed you, we’re up on a roof. What’s going on with yours?”
  3. The text conversation qualifies the job: leak or full replacement, address, insurance claim or cash, how urgent.
  4. The inspection is booked into your calendar. You get a summary text with the details.
  5. Stalled estimates get follow-up automatically, so quotes you already drove out for don't go cold.
